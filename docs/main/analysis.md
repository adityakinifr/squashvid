# Match analysis (squashvid/analysis)

Run: `python -I -m squashvid.analysis <stem>|--all [--no-ball] [--no-review] [--pooled]`.
Evaluate: `python -I -m squashvid.analysis.evaluate`.
Outputs per clip in `work/<stem>/analysis/`: `rallies.parquet`, `hits.parquet`, `shots.parquet` and `endings.parquet` (see [shots.md](shots.md)), `stats.json`, `insights.json`, `report.md`. Pooled outputs in `work/analysis/`. Review plots in `work/analysis_review/`.

## What it computes
- **Hits**: candidates are per-player wrist-speed peaks, audio front-wall onsets with no pose peak, and ball racket events. A Viterbi pass picks a sequence that favours alternating players, front-wall support after the hit, a rival swing, and a still hitter. With ball events present (`work/<stem>/ball_events.parquet`) they are used automatically and hit times snap to the ball's racket event.
- **Rallies**: a per-second play score (logistic on player speeds, swing rate, impact rate, inter-player distance, optional ball presence) with hysteresis, then chained and filtered (at least 2 shots, both players hitting). A gap of more than 2.8 s between shots ends a rally only if the play score also drops below 0.7 inside the gap (gaps over 6 s always do): missed shots in continuous play leave such gaps. Trailing shots where both players move at walking pace (faster under 1.5 m/s and slower under 0.8 m/s, averaged over ±1 s) are dropped first: after a rally the players walk, pick up the ball and bounce it before the serve, and those touches passed as shots. Start is first hit minus 0.6 s, end last hit plus 0.9 s.
- **Serves** start rallies the play score misses (short rallies never build it up). A touch is serve-shaped when the hitter stands still in a service box (within 0.6 m of it across, 0.3 m behind to 0.7 m in front) and the opponent waits at least 1.2 m further back on the other half. While waiting the server bounces the ball with the racket, and those touches have the same shape, so a serve is confirmed only by the ball reaching the front wall (a ball event or audio onset) with the server's last touch before that impact, made from the box (the server may be stepping into it), within 1.6 s of it; or, when the front-wall impact went unseen, by a side-wall impact 0.6-2 s later and a reply by the opponent. hits.parquet carries the result as `serve`. A confirmed serve more than 2 s after the previous shot starts a new rally, and from each serve no rally holds, the shot chain is followed into a rally of its own (or joined to the rally it runs into). The tail trim keeps a serve-led rally's first two shots.
- Front-wall and racket impacts (ball events and audio) mark shots the hit detector missed: a gap they bridge into steps of at most 2.8 s does not end a rally, as long as the play score stays above 0.5. A stretch whose detected hits are all one player's is practice unless two front-wall impacts at least 0.8 s apart fall between two of that player's hits (the opponent hit in between, unseen).
- **Servers and winners** (`serves.py`): the server of each rally is the hitter of a confirmed serve among its first two hits, or, when the serve was missed, the player standing in a service box with the opponent behind on the other half in the two seconds before the first detected hit. The side is the half the server stood on. Nobody records the score, so the winner of rally k is taken from squash's rule that the winner serves next: the server of rally k+1. A server who wins changes sides, so the same server on the same side means a let or an undetected rally; that rally has no winner, unless a gap of more than 30 s (a game break, when the server picks the side) lies between. A clip's last rally has no winner (the three clips were filmed 77 s to 4 minutes apart). `rallies.parquet` gains `server`, `server_side`, `server_source` (serve, setup, unknown), `winner` and `winner_basis`.
  Checked by eye on one frame per rally: all 65 servers found were right (one rally, IMG_4091 at 165 s, has none: the camera was knocked). Winners were inferred for 57 of the 65 rallies. The first game of IMG_4089 gives a check: B wins 10 rallies to A's 4 (one unclear) before the game break and then serves first in game 2, as a game's winner does. The ball-based endings in `shots.py` agree with the inferred winner in 4 of the 7 rallies both decide; in the three others the server sequence (who served next, from which side) is consistent and the ball call is not.
- **Per-hit context**: T distance of both players, available time before the shot, distance the returner moved, 3x3 court cells, recovery time to within 1.5 m of the T, and a rule-of-thumb situation label (approximates the six Murray situations; no centroids available).
- **Stats**: per player, inside rallies only. Every headline number carries n and a 90% bootstrap interval over rallies.
- **Posture at the strike** (`posture.py`): each hit gets `hip_drop` in `hits.parquet`, from the 3D figures (`docs/main/bodies.md`): the lowest pelvis within 0.2 s of the hit, as a share below the player's standing pelvis height (the 95th percentile over the clip). It is left empty when the hitter's figure was seen in under 60% of that window. Height is the best-observed axis of the lift (the camera sees it; depth is the lifter's guess), so it is the only pose number the coaching uses. Checked by eye on crops at hit moments across the three clips: under 0.08 is a player hitting with nearly straight legs, about 0.15 to 0.3 a bent-knee shot or lunge, over 0.45 a scramble on the floor. The most upright "hits" were mostly serves or hits given to the wrong player, so the coaching leaves out each rally's first shot. Pooled, on shots from behind the service boxes, B hit almost upright on 32% (24 of 75) and A on 14% (15 of 107); the gap appears in every clip (39/27/24% against 19/10/8%).
- **Insights**: candidates compared player against opponent, early against late, recovery after short against long available time, and two situations: getting back up to the T after a shot from the back of the court, and how often the next shot is rushed when a player is more than 2 m from the T as the opponent hits. A candidate is suppressed when its 90% interval crosses zero, when it rests on fewer than 8 rallies, or when the gap is below its metric's `min_effect` (the smallest difference worth coaching, e.g. 0.3 m of distance from the T or 8 percentage points of time on it).
- **Coaching** (`coach.py`), told twice. For the player: at most three things to try next time and two to keep doing. Each has a plain sentence written to the player ("you"), with shares said as everyday fractions ("about 1 in 3 of your shots there; Raul about 1 in 7", from `share_words`) and the fixed coaching line after it; the plain sentence passes the verifier like any other, and the item falls back to the fixed line when the candidate lacks a number the sentence needs. For a coach or parent: why it matters (the numbers, and for position items what being away from the T cost), the clip moments to watch and what to look at in them, one drill, and how sure (clear when the whole interval clears `min_effect`, else likely). Findings are grouped into themes (getting back to the T, length, fewer mistakes at the end of rallies, taking time away, getting down to the ball, keeping it up to the end) and each theme gives at most one item per kind. A theme never appears as both a thing to try and a thing to keep, except a narrow thing to try beside a broad thing to keep (holding the T well overall but not after shots from the back). Every sentence with a number cites its candidates and passes the verifier, which rejects any number, timestamp or clip name not in them and bans technique claims, won/lost claims and elite-norm comparisons. A candidate built on the inferred winners lifts the won/lost ban for the sentences that cite it, and every item resting on them says in its detail that winners are inferred from who served next. A posture candidate (`upright_at_back`, theme: getting down to the ball) compares how often each player hit from behind the service boxes almost upright (`hip_drop` under 0.08); in mid-court a volley is rightly hit upright, so only the back of the court counts. Its items say in their detail that the poses are estimated from one camera. Two candidates rest on the winners: how the rallies that ended on a player's shot went (`last_shot_error`, a theme of its own: fewer mistakes at the end of rallies), and how often a player won the rallies in which they held the T more than the opponent (`won_on_T`, told as a reason in the position items). The reports add a "How rallies ended (inferred)" table: rallies won, serving and receiving, in shorter and longer rallies, and the rallies that ended on each player's shot. Pooled over the three clips: B won 37 of 57, A 20; serving made little difference (A won 35% either way); the rallies that ended on A's shot went to B 16 times of 24, those on B's shot to A 12 times of 33; the player who held the T more won 38 of 56.
- **Names**: generated text names the players `{A}` and `{B}`. The report and the viewer fill in the names saved in `work/viewer_settings.json` (the viewer's Names button; A is the light-blue shirt, B the dark shirt in every clip so far), so renaming needs no re-run.

## How far to trust it (IMG_4089, 4090, 4091)
Ground truth is thin; treat all numbers as indicative.
- Hit labels: 78 racket labels from Claude agents in labelled spans only. No ball events, pooled at +-0.2 s: precision 0.68, recall 0.68, hitter accuracy 0.93. Pose-only: P 0.62, R 0.58. Timing MAD about 0.05-0.07 s.
- With ball events (IMG_4091 only, preliminary): +-0.2 s precision 0.79, recall 0.85, hitter accuracy 1.0, timing MAD 0.02 s (no ball: 0.74 / 0.70 / 0.93).
- Hand-labelled ball frames gave only 34 contacts: recall 0.59, hitter accuracy 0.75. Too few to conclude.
- Rallies against a by-eye census (clips 1 and 3; partly used to fit the play score, so optimistic), with ball events: IMG_4091 P 0.94, R 0.67, IoU 0.68, end error median +1.2 s (audio-chain baseline P 0.78, R 0.58, IoU 0.49); IMG_4090 P 0.93, R 0.93, IoU 0.71, end error -0.1 s (baseline 0.43 / 0.60 / 0.48). The walking-pace thresholds were read off these same two clips, so these are in-sample. IMG_4089 has no census.
  Serves and impact links (above) left these scores unchanged except start error (IMG_4090 -0.15 → -0.37 s, IMG_4091 -0.56 → -0.59 s: rallies now begin at the serve, which the census often placed late; by eye, IMG_4091 rally 1 is served by B at 7.9 s, not by A at 9.2 s). On IMG_4089, checked by eye over the whole clip, all 29 rallies found before were real and 5 were missed (86.5, 350.2, 438.6, 463.7 and 478.5 s); now 33 are found, the 438.6 s one from 440 s (its serve and A's shots went undetected), and only 463.7 s is still missed (no front-wall impact was detected and the play score never rose above 0.34). Rally 22's serve at 490.5 s, missed before, now starts it.
  The census is rough: it was written from 1 s contact sheets of the WhatsApp copies and several of its rallies start 0.4-1.4 s after the previous one ends, which a real rally cannot (the server has to fetch the ball). Merging those gives 16 and 12 rallies, and the scores become IMG_4091 P 0.88, R 0.94 and IMG_4090 P 0.73, R 0.92. Checked by eye on the originals: the census is right that IMG_4090 293-298 s is one rally (the detector used to split it) and that IMG_4091 rally 1 ends near 15 s (walking and pre-serve ball bouncing carried it to 19.8 s; with the tail trim it ends at 17.8 s, one touch at 16.9 s being just above walking pace).
- Rally boundaries are typically within about 2-3 s.

## Caveats
- Foot positions are unverified against tape marks; front-court depth is coarse.
- Rally winners are inferred from who served next, not recorded: a let replayed with the same server on the other side, a rally the detector missed, or a player forgetting to change sides puts a rally on the wrong side. A rally that ended on a player's shot and went to the other player is mostly that shot's mistake, but with hit recall about 0.87 it is sometimes the opponent's missed final shot. Distance covered and speed are still reported but not interpreted.
- Situation rules are approximate.
- Ball metrics (height bands, thirds, near-tin, bounce length) exist only where ball events exist (IMG_4091 so far).
- The pooled report assumes player A/B identity is consistent across clips.
- Posture comes from a pose lifted from one camera: it says how low the hips were, not how the knees, trunk or racket moved, and a hit given to the wrong player, or timed a few frames off during a fast move, can put a shot on the wrong side of the 0.08 line. A few shots behind the service boxes are volleys, which may rightly be hit upright.
- The coaching thresholds (`min_effect`, 2 m from the T, the rushed-shot situations, 0.08 for almost upright) were chosen by judgement, not fitted, and the drills are standard junior drills, not prescribed from the data.
